# Final story lock

## Three-sentence story

1. Independent Graph-ANNS builds can change the safe budget required by the same query, so a policy calibrated on a source build cannot be assumed to transport unchanged.
2. When build environments are observationally indistinguishable but require conflicting useful actions, every environment-blind policy must pay in under-budget risk, conservative computation, fallback, or abstention; fixed-target recovery requires endpoint feasibility, identifiability, margin, independent target evidence, and a valid fallback.
3. The phenomenon appears in hnswlib HNSW, Faiss HNSW, and DiskANN3/Vamana-style construction on two datasets, while attempted recovery routes fail at distinct predicted bottlenecks: candidate attainability, structural surrogacy, observable triggering, or economic value.

## Narrative arc

The paper begins with a familiar deployment assumption: rebuilding an index with unchanged data and hyperparameters is treated as operationally interchangeable for downstream query-budget control. The theory isolates build identity as a hidden algorithmic environment and shows why a source policy cannot be uniformly nontrivial when observable transcripts fail to distinguish conflicting target needs. The empirical study then demonstrates that the premise matters across two construction paradigms. Finally, ICBA turns the theory into a certified audit: it diagnoses whether reuse is valid, whether target evidence is needed, and whether a safe decision has economic value. The method-search history is compressed into a four-rung falsification ladder showing that each route failed where the theory says it could fail.

## Positioning

## Scope sentence

The evidence covers registered builds, frozen query distributions, two datasets, three implementations, and two construction paradigms; the positive guarantee is fixed-target and finite-action, not an unseen-build or open-world certificate.
