\section{A finite-environment overlap--separation lower bound}
Let $b_j(z)\in[0,1]$ denote a deterministic normalized sufficient-budget
response on a common visible state space and
$\ell_j(a,z)=\lambda(b_j(z)-a)_+ +(a-b_j(z))_+$.
For two environments suppose the measurable set
$A_\Delta=\{z:b_1(z)-b_0(z)\ge\Delta\}$ has
$P_j(A_\Delta)\ge\rho$ and $b_1-b_0\ge\Delta$ on that set. Then every
possibly randomized policy measurable with respect to $Z$ satisfies
\[
 \inf_\pi\sup_{j\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb E_j\ell_j(\pi(Z),U)
 \ge {\min\{\lambda,1\}\Delta\over2}
       [\rho-\operatorname{TV}(P_0^Z,P_1^Z)]_+ .
\]
Indeed, on the common part $\mu=P_0^Z\wedge P_1^Z$ and on
$A_\Delta$, for every realized action,
\[
 (a-b_0)_++\lambda(b_1-a)_+
 \ge \min\{\lambda,1\}(b_1-b_0)
 \ge \min\{\lambda,1\}\Delta.
\]
Moreover $\mu(A_\Delta)\ge[\rho-\mathrm{TV}]_+$. Integration bounds
the sum of the two risks, division by two bounds their average, and the
maximum dominates the average. The pointwise inequality also proves the
randomized-policy case. Right-censored budgets require an independently
valid lower interval for $\Delta$ and may not be replaced by the maximum
observed grid point.
