# Claim Rules for the Falsification Ladder

## Allowed

- The attempted methods test distinct assumptions of the conditional recovery framework.
- Fixed-target safety can be restored with independent target evidence, without implying economic value.
- The tested CIBS candidate pool lacked a jointly CI-supported action across both datasets.
- The tested structural operators did not establish a structure-to-native-budget bridge.
- Portal complementarity is a replicated mechanism signal under corrected ID semantics.
- The tested truth-free triggers and shared-cost regime did not make portal rescue deployable.
- The certified auditor can avoid uncertified acceptance by falling back or abstaining.
- Safe auditing and decision value are different objectives.

## Required qualifiers

- Use “the tested route/action/signal family,” not “all methods.”
- Use “fixed target” for every positive certificate.
- Label per-query Oracles and ideal candidate unions as non-deployable bounds.
- Separate point risk, confidence bounds, mean NDC, p95/p99, offline cost, and break-even.
- State that a route failure does not prove a universal impossibility result.

## Prohibited

- The project found a SOTA recovery algorithm.
- Target recalibration is universally efficient.
- CIBS proves build selection cannot work.
- Stable-by-construction is impossible.
- Portal rescue is safe or economical merely because it rescues failures.
- Zero unsafe acceptance proves positive deployment value.
- Retrospective Oracle headroom constitutes an observable method.
- Failed algorithms prove Main Theorem I or Main Theorem II.
- The ladder authorizes new compute on a closed route.
