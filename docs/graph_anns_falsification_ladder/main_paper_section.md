# Main-Paper Draft: Theory-Guided Falsification of Recovery Routes

The lower bound identifies hidden build information as a barrier, while the conditional recovery theorem specifies what a successful fixed-target route must supply. We therefore evaluate recovery attempts as a falsification ladder rather than as an unconstrained algorithm tournament. Each family targets a different missing condition: source reuse tests portability; recalibration tests whether target evidence restores a safe margin; build selection and construction test candidate attainability and structural sufficiency; additive lanes test failure complementarity and observability; and certified auditing tests whether statistical safety produces decision value. A route advances only if its mechanism, safety, robustness, tail, and economic premises survive in that order.

**Reuse and recalibration.** A source policy did not transport reliably as a nontrivial rule. Target-only residual recalibration appeared to reduce mean search cost by approximately 29.40% on SIFT and 26.17% on Arxiv in a paired fixed-target design while passing its registered safety checks. However, history-assisted variants added 0.90% (95% interval −8.73% to 11.25%) and −0.13% (−9.24% to 10.26%) beyond target-only recalibration. Under strict disjoint-query cross-fitting, the apparent target-only improvement became −7.35% and −12.49%. Subsequent attribution tied most of the earlier gain to margin and query-reuse effects. We retain the positive conclusion that independent target evidence can certify a named target, but reject a stable incremental-value or source-history mechanism.

**Build selection and stable construction.** Safe selection can help only if the candidate pool contains a jointly safe, low-cost action. Across 72 frozen build–budget actions, SIFT had zero point-estimate or interval-supported jointly feasible actions, while Arxiv had one point-estimate action and zero interval-supported actions. Stable-construction attempts did not repair this reachability failure: one repair lowered mean NDC from 509.49 to 503.41 and p95 from 849 to 833, but reduced Recall@10 by 0.002 and worsened both expansion- and native-budget endpoints. A fully bridged structural operator subsequently reduced recall by 0.0158/0.0042 at low/mid budgets and increased mean and tail NDC. These results falsify the tested candidate and surrogate families, not the abstract possibility of stable construction.

**Additive lanes and portal rescue.** Corrected ID semantics revealed real complementarity. Fixed portal sets transferred with positive rescue on all registered builds, producing 1,064 and 727 additional hits and 426 and 348 threshold rescues on SIFT and Arxiv, respectively. Yet the combined action remained below matched single-lane recall by 4.79 and 3.29 percentage points and cost approximately 2.77× and 2.47× as much. Eight preregistered truth-free trigger rules produced no positive validation advantage, and a shared-frontier audit showed that auxiliary work would need 79.5%–93.4% compression before quality-matched cost competitiveness. Thus conditional rescue is genuine but insufficient without an observable trigger and realizable cost sharing.

**Certified auditing and fallback.** The final family tests whether conservative risk control itself yields a useful action. After runtime semantic repair, the auditor never labeled an uncertified action safe. In the complete fixed-target audit, 60 decisions produced 4 direct reuses, 32 certified fallbacks, and 24 abstentions; all 36 accepted decisions were safe. Nevertheless every accepted action executed the maximum registered budget, yielding no advantage over always falling back and positive search-only regret relative to the target-safe Oracle: 470.34 (95% interval 458.69–482.03) on SIFT and 684.49 (674.99–694.26) on Arxiv. The auditor therefore succeeds as a safety-preserving diagnostic but fails as a performance optimizer.

Together these results locate the unresolved positive problem. The main barrier is not a single deficient model: different routes fail at different interfaces—source portability, candidate reachability, structural sufficiency, observable triggering, or cost. This supports ICBA’s use as an auditing procedure and explains why the paper does not claim a deployable recovery algorithm.

## Main-paper table

| Recovery hypothesis | Theoretical condition | Strongest positive evidence | Falsifying evidence | Final inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reuse or recalibrate | identifiable target and positive margin | named-target safety can be restored | history adds no stable value; strict splits remove the apparent gain | fixed-target safety only |
| Select or construct a build | attainable action and valid response bridge | isolated mean/tail improvements | no jointly feasible certified pool; recall/native budgets worsen | tested selection/construction route closed |
| Add a rescue lane | conditional rescue, observable trigger, realizable shared cost | portal complementarity replicates | no deployable trigger; 79.5%–93.4% compression needed | mechanism only |
| Certify and fallback | valid certificate/fallback plus decision value | zero unsafe acceptance is achievable | maximum-budget fallback/abstention and positive regret | auditor method, not optimizer |
