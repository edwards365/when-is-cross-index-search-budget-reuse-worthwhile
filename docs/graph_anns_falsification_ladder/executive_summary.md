# Theory-Guided Falsification Ladder — Executive Summary

## Decision

`THEORY_GUIDED_FALSIFICATION_LADDER_LOCK_PASS`

The algorithm-search history is now organized as a sequence of falsifiable recovery hypotheses rather than a catalogue of failed implementations. The ladder begins with the phenomenon established by the cross-family evidence—independent builds alter query-level safe budgets—and asks, in order, whether a recovery route has a feasible action, observable target-relevant information, a valid safety bridge, independent certification, and positive deployment value.

Four method families are retained. Source reuse and target recalibration show that target evidence can restore fixed-target safety, while strict role separation and matched baselines remove a stable claim of incremental economic value. Certified build selection and stable construction show that safe selection cannot create an absent jointly feasible action and that graph/trace proxies do not generally control native budget response. Additive portal lanes establish genuine conditional rescue, but no truth-free trigger or realizable shared cost. The certified auditor controls unsafe acceptance through certification, fallback, or abstention, yet often collapses to the maximum budget and therefore has no decision value.

The cumulative inference is stronger than “several algorithms failed.” Each route is pruned at a distinct condition predicted by the theory: portability, candidate attainability, observable identifiability, structural sufficiency, valid certification, or economics. No closed route is authorized for further compute. The surviving method contribution is ICBA as a certified diagnostic procedure that determines where a proposed recovery route fails and prevents unsafe or economically unsupported claims.

The main paper should contain one compact ladder table, one route-pruning figure, and four short case paragraphs. Internal method codes, detailed gates, and secondary numbers belong in the appendix and artifact.
