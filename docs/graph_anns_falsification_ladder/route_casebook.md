# Route Casebook

## Family 1 — Source reuse and target recalibration

### Hypothesis chain

1. A source policy remains safe and useful after rebuild.
2. If not, a small independent target sample restores a positive safety margin.
3. Historical builds provide incremental information beyond target-only calibration.
4. Certification and fallback preserve net value under strict role separation.

### Audit result

Step 1 fails as a general nontrivial portability claim. Step 2 passes only for named targets and selected experimental designs. Step 3 fails: the history increment is small with intervals spanning zero. Step 4 fails in the strict cross-fit and matched-risk analyses. The family is retained to demonstrate the distinction between “target evidence can make a decision safe” and “a new method creates additional value.”

### Paper use

Main text: one paired-versus-disjoint contrast and the history increment interval. Appendix: the full B1/B2/B5, ASRC, margin attribution, and auditor crosswalk. Do not expose internal codes in the abstract or introduction.

## Family 2 — Build selection and stable construction

### Hypothesis chain

1. Randomly generated builds contain a safe, low-cost candidate.
2. Simultaneous certification can select it.
3. If the pool is inadequate, a structural operator can create a better candidate.
4. The structural proxy controls the actual implementation-native budget response.

### Audit result

The selection theorem remains correct, but candidate attainability fails for the frozen portfolio. Stable-construction operators preserve several graph invariants yet fail the native search-response gate. This is a clean separation between valid statistical selection and an inadequate action generator.

### Paper use

Main text: the 72-action joint-feasibility result and one corrected structural smoke result. Appendix: CFSR-Lite, GSC O4/O6, protected-edge invariants, tracer equivalence, and the semantic downgrade from expansion-prefix reasoning to empirical raw-budget calibration.

## Family 3 — Additive lanes and portal rescue

### Hypothesis chain

1. An auxiliary lane rescues failures of the primary lane.
2. A fixed or observable rule identifies useful portals without target truth.
3. Shared search state makes rescue cheaper than executing independent lanes.
4. The union satisfies absolute safety, recall noninferiority, tails, and economics.

### Audit result

Step 1 passes after correcting the external-label/internal-table mapping. Fixed portal sets transfer directionally across registered builds. Steps 2–4 fail: no truth-free trigger passes, the independent multi-entry implementation is expensive, recall remains lower than the matched baseline, and the required shared-work compression is implausibly high under the registered 50% gate.

### Paper use

Main text: corrected rescue counts, cost multiples, and required compression. Appendix: all 256 subsets, LOBO, ID-repair audit, trigger rules, and candidate-union Oracle lower bound.

## Family 4 — Certified auditor, fallback, and abstention

### Hypothesis chain

1. Runtime semantics implement the theorem assumptions.
2. A frozen candidate is certified on independent target evidence.
3. Rejection invokes a genuinely safe fallback; otherwise the method abstains.
4. Conservative decisions improve on always-fallback and target profiling.

### Audit result

Steps 1–3 can be implemented conservatively after abandoning raw-action nesting assumptions and uncertified maximum-budget fallback. Step 4 fails: accepted decisions collapse to the maximum registered action, so the procedure preserves safety without improving decision regret or economics.

### Paper use

Main text: the 60-decision action distribution, zero unsafe acceptance, and regret intervals. Appendix: semantic patches, Bonferroni/Clopper–Pearson details, missing-cost states, and all abstention reasons.

## Cumulative conclusion

No route satisfies the complete deployment chain under its frozen action and signal family. The sequence nevertheless supplies a coherent scientific result: the negative theorem identifies the information barrier, the positive theorem specifies conditional escape routes, and the ladder shows which condition each concrete method lacks. This justifies stopping algorithm search and writing the current paper around non-portability, conditional recovery, and certified diagnosis.
