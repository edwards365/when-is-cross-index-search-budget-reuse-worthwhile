# Theory-Guided Falsification Ladder — Full Report

## 1. Task outcome

The historical algorithm work has been transformed into a theory-guided falsification ladder with four method families, sixteen route-level records, sixteen registered recovery conditions, a main-paper table, an appendix casebook, a condition-coverage matrix, an evidence registry, and a publication figure specification.

Unique decision: `THEORY_GUIDED_FALSIFICATION_LADDER_LOCK_PASS`.

No new ANN search, index construction, model training, query/truth access, or threshold tuning was performed. No closed algorithm route is reopened or authorized for additional compute.

## 2. Why a ladder is scientifically preferable

A chronological “we tried many methods” section would invite the interpretation that the project is an unsuccessful algorithm search. The ladder instead begins from the two locked theoretical results. The hidden-environment theorem explains why source-only recovery requires informative transcripts. The finite-class recovery theorem identifies endpoint feasibility, identifiability, positive margin, independent target evidence, and valid fallback/abstention as recovery conditions. Concrete methods then become tests of these conditions.

This organization preserves negative and partial-positive results without claiming that experiments prove the theorems. It also makes stopping decisions principled: once a route fails an upstream condition such as candidate attainability or observable triggering, larger downstream benchmarking cannot repair the scientific defect.

## 3. Ladder levels

### Level 0 — Semantic validity

The action must be native to the implementation, the endpoint-aware event must be shared across selection and evaluation, unresolved states remain categorical, query roles are disjoint, and all costs are named. This level removed invalid raw-`ef` monotonicity, ordered-rung, and uncertified fallback interpretations.

### Level 1 — Attainability and information

The registered action family must contain a nontrivial safe candidate, and a deployment-visible transcript must identify the appropriate action. CIBS failed on candidate attainability; source reuse and portal triggering failed on deployable information.

### Level 2 — Safety bridge

The mechanism must preserve or control the endpoint-aware failure event. Structural similarity and protected edges did not control native budget response. Additive completed-primary union has a restricted mechanical preservation result, but this alone does not make the auxiliary lane safe or cheap.

### Level 3 — Independent certification

Candidate selection and final certification are separated, or all adaptive multiplicity is controlled. Raw actions use Bonferroni/simultaneous bounds unless population-level nesting is independently proved. Rejection uses an independently safe fallback or abstains.

### Level 4 — Deployment value

Safety, mean cost, p95/p99, robustness, and total break-even are evaluated independently. Missing truth, retraining, or control costs remain `NOT_ESTIMABLE`. A method cannot pass merely by saving mean work while degrading quality or tails.

## 4. Four family decisions

### 4.1 Reuse and target recalibration

The family establishes the narrow positive result that target evidence can restore fixed-target safety. It does not establish portable source-policy reuse, a stable benefit from history, or net deployment value. The decisive contrast is between the paired recalibration gain and its disappearance under strict disjoint cross-fitting and matched-risk attribution.

Final family verdict: `PASS_FIXED_TARGET_SAFETY_ONLY`; closed as a novel recovery optimizer.

### 4.2 Build selection and stable construction

The statistical selection rule is not the failure: the frozen candidate family lacks a jointly CI-supported action. Attempts to generate improved builds through protected-edge, trace, or behavioral surrogates fail the native response/Recall Pareto gate after semantic bridging.

Final family verdict: `FAIL_ATTAINABILITY_AND_SEMANTIC_BRIDGE`; closed for the tested candidate generators.

### 4.3 Multi-lane and portal rescue

After correcting ID semantics, portal complementarity is real and transfers across registered builds. The route fails because rescue cannot be selected from a truth-free transcript and cannot be implemented economically under the registered cost regime. Required auxiliary compression of 79.5%–93.4% is beyond the preregistered realizability threshold.

Final family verdict: `PASS_MECHANISM_ONLY`; closed as a deployable portal method.

### 4.4 Certified auditor and fallback

The auditor can maintain the invariant that no uncertified action is called safe. Nevertheless, certificates, fallback, and abstention frequently collapse to the maximum budget. In the complete fixed-target audit, every accepted action used `ef=200`, leaving positive regret relative to the target-safe Oracle.

Final family verdict: `PASS_SAFETY_DIAGNOSTIC_FAIL_OPTIMIZATION`; retained as the ICBA method contribution.

## 5. Cumulative theoretical interpretation

The experiments expose three distinct gaps that must not be conflated:

1. **Oracle–observable gap:** a beneficial per-query or retrospective action may exist without a deployable signal.
2. **Observable–certifiable gap:** a predictor may correlate with difficulty without providing enough margin or valid independent evidence for a certificate.
3. **Certifiable–valuable gap:** a safe decision may require enough evidence, fallback, or computation that it has no mean, tail, or break-even advantage.

Candidate attainability precedes all three. A safe selection theorem cannot produce value if no registered action satisfies the joint target. These gaps explain the repeated method failures without changing the validity of the negative theorem or the conditional positive theorem.

## 7. Claim boundary

The ladder supports the claim that multiple recovery strategies fail at different theoretically predicted prerequisites within their frozen families. It does not support the claim that every recovery algorithm is impossible, that the attempted methods prove the lower bound, or that ICBA is a SOTA search algorithm. A future method that changes the action space or observable channel is a new scientific route and requires a new preregistration; it is not authorized here.
