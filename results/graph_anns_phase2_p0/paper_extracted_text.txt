When Safe Search Budgets Do Not Transfer Across Graph-ANNS Rebuilds
Anonymous Authors
Abstract
Graph approximate nearest-neighbor systems rebuild indexes after data refreshes, maintenance, and randomized construction, yet downstream search-budget policies are commonly reused across builds. We study when this transport is statistically justified. Each serialized build is modeled as an algorithmic environment with an implementation-native finite action set, an endpoint-aware failure event, and a categorical unresolved state. A transcript-limited two-environment reduction shows that, when an available probe transcript cannot distinguish builds requiring conflicting useful actions, any build-blind policy must incur unsafe execution, conservative computation, fallback, or abstention in at least one environment. A complementary fixed-target theorem gives sufficient conditions for safe recovery: endpoint feasibility, action-relevant identifiability, positive risk margin, independent target evidence, and a valid fallback or abstention rule. We operationalize these results through ICBA, a certified portability audit, and a deterministic-rebuild contract. Across SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K, strictly comparable hnswlib and Faiss HNSW experiments exhibit 75.87%–91.07% finite-action safe-budget variation and 17.17%–23.60% incremental transport risk. Vamana-style experiments independently support the direction (11.37%–16.86%) under a stage-specific estimand. Fixing input order, seed, thread count, and toolchain eliminates measured transport risk, with 1.71×–3.52× build-time overhead. Primitive profiling is cheap at 100K scale and operator dependent; therefore we claim neither universal profiling cost nor open-world recovery. An anonymized artifact accompanies the submission.
1 Introduction
Graph-based approximate nearest-neighbor search (Graph-ANNS) is often tuned as though an index were a stable substrate. After rebuilding with unchanged data and nominal hyperparameters, practitioners may reuse a global budget, query-level predictor, or stopping rule selected on an earlier index. The statistical premise behind this practice is rarely stated: the policy is assumed to remain safe and useful under a new realization of the construction algorithm. Reconfiguration can violate that premise through input order, randomized state, parallel scheduling, graph pruning, software version, or build configuration—even when the query and data distributions do not change.
This issue is not simply a drop in mean recall. Two builds can have similar aggregate quality while assigning different minimum safe actions to the same query. Reusing a source action can then under-budget a target build; choosing a uniformly larger action can avoid some failures while paying conservative computation. Existing adaptive search, cost prediction, and risk-control methods are valuable for a profiled target index, but their calibration does not automatically transport across an independently rebuilt index. Conversely, target profiling is not uniformly expensive: our 100K-scale primitive measurements are sub-second to about one second. The scientific gap is therefore the validity and decision semantics of transport, not a blanket claim that profiling is always prohibitive.
We formulate build reconfiguration as a finite environment–action problem. For each serialized build and query, the safe budget is the smallest registered implementation-native action satisfying a quality event. If no registered action succeeds, we retain a categorical unresolved symbol rather than imputing a larger numerical budget. Directed transport separates four outcomes that are frequently conflated: unsafe execution, conservative computation, certified fallback, and abstention. This separation turns an informal robustness concern into an auditable risk–cost object.
Our theoretical contribution separates impossibility from conditional recovery. The negative result is transcript limited: for a fixed probe budget and observable transcript, two environments that are difficult to distinguish yet require conflicting useful actions induce a nonzero deployment loss for any build-blind rule. It does not assert that every pair of builds is intrinsically hidden. The positive result is fixed-target and finite: endpoint feasibility, action-relevant information, a positive risk margin, independent target evidence, and a valid fallback or abstention rule suffice for safe screening and certification. The testing inequalities are classical; the contribution is their build-conditioned Graph-ANNS formulation and operational composition.
We instantiate the theory in ICBA, a certified audit rather than a new traversal algorithm. ICBA validates action semantics and role separation, measures cross-build response heterogeneity, evaluates directed transport, and certifies only frozen fixed-target actions. It reports mean, tail, abstention, and break-even quantities separately. The audit yields two actionable outcomes: either remove the environment by enforcing and replay-checking a deterministic build contract, or treat the target build as a new environment and collect target evidence. A certificate cannot create a useful action when the candidate family contains none.
The main empirical evidence comprises four strictly comparable HNSW data–implementation cells: hnswlib and Faiss on SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K. Finite-action safe-budget variation is 75.87%–91.07%, and incremental transport risk is 17.17%–23.60% with all confidence intervals above the preregistered 2% materiality threshold. Two Vamana-style cells independently reproduce the risk direction (11.37%–16.86%) under their original stage-specific endpoint estimand, but are not promoted to harmonized equality. Deterministic single-thread rebuilding reduces measured transport risk to zero in registered controls while increasing build time by 1.71×–3.52×. Thus risk transport replicates across registered families, whereas cost and mitigation trade-offs remain operator dependent.
Our contributions are:
We define build-reconfiguration portability using implementation-native actions, categorical unresolved endpoints, and four distinct deployment outcomes.
We give a transcript-limited lower bound and a fixed-target conditional recovery theorem, explicitly separating impossibility, evidence, certification, and fallback assumptions.
We introduce ICBA as a certified diagnostic workflow and derive a deterministic-rebuild contract that eliminates the measured environment variation under registered conditions.
We provide clean cross-implementation HNSW evidence on two datasets and supporting Vamana-style evidence, with estimand, uncertainty, and comparability boundaries stated explicitly.
We organize extensive negative method evidence as a theory-guided falsification ladder, identifying whether each route fails at attainability, observability, certification, tail behavior, or economics.

2 Related Work
2.1 Graph-based ANNS construction and search
HNSW uses a randomized multilayer proximity graph and greedy candidate-list search (Malkov & Yashunin, 2020). NSG approximates a monotonic relative-neighborhood structure (Fu et al., 2019), while DiskANN introduced Vamana-style robust pruning for memory- and disk-efficient search at scale (Subramanya et al., 2019). Faiss provides an independent optimized HNSW implementation (Johnson et al., 2021). Incremental graph maintenance can avoid full rebuilding after updates (Xu et al., 2022), and recent work revisits proximity-graph construction cost and pruning. These systems reduce construction or maintenance cost; our question is complementary: when a build does change, does a previously selected safety-critical search action transport to the realized graph?
2.2 Query-adaptive search and cost prediction
Learned early termination, adaptive exploration, conformal retrieval, and learned query-cost estimators improve efficiency for a concrete profiled index (Li et al., 2020; Chatzakis et al., 2025; Horchidan et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026). They establish that query difficulty is predictable within an environment. Unlike these methods, we do not propose another predictor; we test whether the predictor or budget selected on one serialized build retains its risk and cost semantics after an independent rebuild.
2.3 Risk control and safe selection
Learn-Then-Test, conformal risk control, selective classification, and safe best-arm identification provide finite-sample tools for selecting or abstaining within a fixed candidate family (Angelopoulos et al., 2025; Bates et al., 2021; Angelopoulos et al., 2024; El-Yaniv & Wiener, 2010; Wang et al., 2022). ICBA uses these classical tools only for a named target and frozen actions. Its distinct contribution is to expose when build-conditioned transport invalidates the premise of reuse, and to keep unsafe execution, conservative cost, fallback, and abstention as separate decisions.
2.4 Distribution shift and algorithmic reconfiguration
Domain adaptation theory shows that source performance need not determine target performance without support or structural assumptions (Ben-David et al., 2010; Johansson et al., 2019). Multi-environment predictive inference treats environments as higher-level sampling units (Duchi et al., 2025). Rebuilding an index is neither ordinary covariate shift nor a change in the underlying vector dataset: the query distribution may remain fixed while the algorithmic state changes. We therefore treat each serialized build and its replay semantics as an environment. This framing supports a precise fixed-target guarantee while making clear that query bootstrap conditional on registered builds is not an unseen-build confidence interval.

5 ICBA: Certified Audit and Actionable Mitigation
ICBA receives registered source and target builds, implementation-native actions, disjoint design/selection/certification/evaluation roles, exact truth for authorized labeled roles, one endpoint-aware failure event, implementation-internal costs, risk parameters, and a frozen candidate family. It returns one of four scientifically distinct outcomes: certified reuse, target-specific recalibration/certification, execution of an independently justified fallback, or abstention. It never treats the maximum registered action as safe without evidence.
The procedure first validates role disjointness, replay, native action semantics, and cost fields. It then constructs the categorical safe-budget response without endpoint imputation and measures directed source-to-target transport. Any candidate selected using authorized target data is frozen before independent certification, with familywise control across the registered actions. Finally, ICBA audits mean cost, p95/p99 behavior, offline truth and control cost, fallback rate, and break-even separately. Missing costs remain not estimable rather than being silently replaced by a proxy.
Figure 2. ICBA workflow. The procedure validates semantics before estimating transport, separates selection from certification, and treats fallback and abstention as distinct terminal outcomes.
A complementary mitigation removes, rather than learns, the build environment. The deterministic-rebuild contract fixes the input permutation, construction seed where exposed, thread count, implementation and toolchain, and requires byte-level index replay plus search-output identity checks. Under these registered conditions, the contract mechanically reuses the same serialized environment; it is not an unseen-build guarantee and does not cover data refreshes, version changes, or hardware changes. ICBA therefore implements a simple decision rule: enforce a verified deterministic contract when feasible; otherwise collect target evidence and certify a frozen action; if neither yields a certified action, abstain.
6 Experimental Protocol
We evaluate SIFT-100K (128-dimensional L2 vectors) and Arxiv-Nomic-100K (768-dimensional normalized embeddings under the registered L2/cosine-equivalent preprocessing) at Recall@10 with hit requirement h=10. hnswlib and the clean Faiss-100K experiments contain 24 registered builds per dataset and all 552 non-diagonal directed pairs; the Vamana-style stage contains 12 builds and 36 preregistered source-to-held-out-target directions. Each cell uses 750 evaluation queries. Native action grids are reported per implementation; raw efSearch and l_value values are not equated across systems. For Vamana, beam width is fixed to one, so its evidence is explicitly configuration conditional.
Evaluation queries are unique and content-hash disjoint from the indexed base and historical analysis roles in the clean Faiss-100K rerun. Serialized-index replay, identifier mapping, and native/tracer agreement are checked before inference. No future-replication truth or outcome is used; any hash-only access status is retained in the artifact audit rather than promoted to a scientific claim. Primary intervals use 5,000 query-bootstrap repetitions with seed 991 while retaining each sampled query's full vector of directed-pair observations. The intervals are conditional on the registered builds, not confidence intervals over unseen rebuilds.
The primary estimand is the stage-registered endpoint-aware incremental transport risk. For the four HNSW cells, the clean evidence supports a common source-action convention. The Vamana stage used a different reference construction, and its frozen records cannot reconstruct the fully harmonized HNSW estimand; we therefore report Vamana as supporting stage-specific evidence rather than merge all six cells into one numerical range. We also report finite-action variation separately from endpoint variation, so response heterogeneity cannot be attributed to unresolved endpoints. Costs are compared only within an implementation.
7 Results
7.1 Strictly comparable HNSW transport evidence
The clean hnswlib and Faiss experiments agree on the main conclusion. Across the four data–implementation cells, 75.87%–91.07% of evaluation queries exhibit finite-action safe-budget variation across registered builds, whereas endpoint-state variation is only 0.53%–2.67%. Incremental source-to-target transport risk ranges from 17.17% to 23.60% (Figure 3 and Table 3). Every 95% query-bootstrap interval excludes the preregistered 2% materiality threshold. Thus the primary signal is switching among finite native actions, not a numerical artifact created by unresolved endpoints.

7.2 Supporting Vamana-style scope and operator-dependent cost
The Vamana-style stage reproduces the direction on both datasets: 71.07%/48.27% category variation and 16.86%/11.37% endpoint-aware transport risk on SIFT/Arxiv. However, it uses 12 builds, 36 asymmetric directions, beam width one, and a stage-specific reference construction that cannot be harmonized exactly from the frozen event table. It therefore broadens the construction-family scope without supporting numerical equality to HNSW. Cost is even less universal: hnswlib has resolved within-implementation ROM-NDC penalties of 19.45% and 14.88%, whereas the Vamana estimates are near 1% with intervals crossing zero, and clean Faiss-100K NDC is not estimable because the exposed counter is batch cumulative.
7.3 Deterministic rebuilding removes the registered environment
The order-dominant decomposition suggests an inexpensive conceptual remedy: avoid drawing a new build environment. We therefore fix the input order, seed, thread count, implementation, and toolchain, and require byte-identical serialization and search replay. Three repeated builds per dataset satisfy all identity checks and have measured transport risk zero. Relative to the original multithreaded construction, the contract preserves Recall@10 within 0.0004, leaves p95/p99 safe budget unchanged, and changes mean safe budget by ratios 0.976 (SIFT) and 0.999 (Arxiv), but build time increases by 3.52× and 1.71×. This is an actionable environment-elimination contract, not a recovery algorithm for changed data or software.

7.4 Profiling economics narrows the claim
Primitive target profiling at 100K scale is inexpensive: 59-query measurements range from 0.017 s to 0.994 s across the registered HNSW implementations and datasets. In one Arxiv hnswlib setting, the measured search-only break-even is approximately 11,832 service queries and the broader resident/all-in estimate is approximately 104,383 queries; a selected SIFT setting has no finite break-even because it yields no online saving. These numbers refute a universal “profiling is expensive” premise. They instead show that the choice between deterministic rebuilding, target profiling, and abstention depends on operator, workload, certification family, truth cost, and service horizon. Because a complete candidate-family and control-cost ledger is unavailable, we do not report a universal deployment break-even.
8 Theory-Guided Falsification Ladder
The transcript-limited lower bound identifies insufficient build information as one possible barrier, while Theorem 2 states what a fixed-target escape requires. We therefore organize method development as a falsification ladder rather than a performance tournament. A route advances only if its semantics, candidate attainability, observable information or structural bridge, independent certification, tail behavior, and deployment value survive in that order.

Target-only residual recalibration initially reduced mean NDC by 29.40% on SIFT and 26.17% on Arxiv in a paired fixed-target design. History-assisted variants added only 0.90% (95% interval -8.73% to 11.25%) and -0.13% (-9.24% to 10.26%). Under strict disjoint-query cross-fitting, the target-only effects became -7.35% and -12.49%, showing that target evidence can support a certificate without establishing a stable new recovery mechanism.
Build selection failed earlier in the chain. Among 72 frozen build–budget actions, SIFT had no point-estimate or interval-supported jointly feasible action; Arxiv had one point-estimate action and none with interval support. Stable-construction operators preserved several graph invariants but failed the native response gate: one repair lowered mean NDC from 509.49 to 503.41 and p95 from 849 to 833, yet reduced Recall@10 by 0.002 and worsened both expansion- and native-budget endpoints. This separates a valid selection rule from an inadequate action generator.
After correcting identifier semantics, fixed portal sets showed genuine rescue complementarity across registered builds, adding 1,064 and 727 hits and 426 and 348 threshold rescues on SIFT and Arxiv. However, the multi-lane action cost 2.77× and 2.47× a matched single lane and remained 4.79 and 3.29 recall percentage points lower. Eight preregistered truth-free triggers showed no positive validation advantage; a shared-frontier bound implied that auxiliary work would need 79.5%–93.4% compression before becoming cost competitive.
Finally, the conservative auditor demonstrated safety without value. Across 60 retrospective fixed-target decisions it returned four direct reuses, 32 certified fallbacks, and 24 abstentions; all 36 accepted decisions were safe. Every accepted action nevertheless used the maximum registered budget. Search-only regret relative to a target-safe oracle was 470.34 [458.69, 482.03] on SIFT and 684.49 [674.99, 694.26] on Arxiv. ICBA therefore survives as a safety-preserving audit, not as a performance optimizer.
9 Limitations and Scope
Our primary evidence covers two 100K-scale datasets and four comparable HNSW cells. The Vamana extension uses a different estimand, asymmetric pairs, and beam width one. We do not model future builds, data refreshes, or open-world safety. Scale, pruning, I/O, and hardware may change the effect; clean Faiss NDC and Vamana wall-clock/I/O are not estimable, precluding a universal cost claim.
The lower bound is conditional: our experiments establish response heterogeneity, not a nearly indistinguishable build pair. Instantiating it requires a declared probe class, probe budget, and action-relevant testing distance. The recovery theorem also requires a useful margin-separated action, which the falsification ladder often rejects. The 2% gate is a preregistered scientific threshold, not a deployment utility function.
The deterministic contract eliminates registered same-data variation but costs 1.71×–3.52× more build time and does not cover changed data, software, or operators. At 100K scale, target profiling may be preferable. Any larger-scale advantage remains unverified until truth, control, latency, and I/O costs are measured end to end.
The historical baseline remains conditionally reproducible (111/123 matching entries). Later clean reruns do not erase this limitation. The artifact records hash-only role access, Vamana reference semantics, and the descriptive—not causal—status of construction-factor comparisons.
10 Conclusion
Safe search budgets are properties of a realized Graph-ANNS build, not only of a dataset and nominal recipe. Across two datasets and two HNSW implementations, rebuilds cause large finite-action variation and 17.17%–23.60% incremental transport risk; a Vamana-style study supports the direction under a distinct estimand. The theory separates transcript-limited non-portability from fixed-target recovery. ICBA audits the result, while deterministic rebuilding provides a narrow mitigation at an offline cost. The practical rule is therefore conditional: verify that a rebuild reproduces the registered environment, otherwise treat it as a new target and certify or abstain.

Appendix E Complete Experimental Protocol
The registered unit is a serialized build, not a query row. The strict HNSW evidence uses 24 hnswlib and 24 clean Faiss-100K builds per dataset and all 552 non-diagonal directed pairs; the Vamana-style analysis uses 12 builds and 36 preregistered source-to-held-out-target directions. Every cell contains 750 evaluation queries at Recall@10 with h=10. The clean Faiss query roles have zero ID, raw-content, and normalized-content overlap with the indexed base and historical roles. Native action grids and counters are never numerically equated across implementations.
The query bootstrap resamples query identities while retaining the complete vector of registered directed-pair observations. It quantifies query-distribution uncertainty conditional on the finite build family. LOBO and leave-one-seed/order analyses test domination but do not define an unseen-build population. Top-contributor deletion removes 1% of evaluation queries under a frozen contribution rule. The confidence intervals therefore support registered-family conclusions only.
An unresolved endpoint remains ⊥ in all categorical analyses. The endpoint-aware primary risk preserves each source stage’s reference semantics. The harmonized sensitivity conditions on source and target both having observed safe actions. Mean, p95, p99, build cost, truth cost, control cost, and fallback cost are separate fields; missing items remain not estimable.

Appendix H Theory Conflict Resolution and Claim Registry
The final theory removes several overclaims. Raw efSearch is not assumed to generate nested population failure events. Structural similarity does not guarantee budget stability. Query bootstrap is not build-level inference. A maximum registered action is not a safe fallback without evidence. Mean savings do not imply tail savings. The negative result is transcript limited rather than an absolute hidden-build theorem, and neither main theorem is a new general Le Cam, KL, Learn-Then-Test, or best-arm result.
Permitted claims are restricted to registered-family HNSW evidence, stage-specific supporting Vamana evidence, fixed-target certification, the deterministic same-environment contract, and operator-dependent cost. Prohibited claims include universal Graph-ANNS behavior, open-world recovery, unseen-build safety, native-budget numerical equivalence, universal profiling or conservative-cost conclusions, and production/SOTA performance.
