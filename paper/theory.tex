\section{Local resistance--direction neighbor selection}
\label{sec:theory}

We distinguish the directed HNSW search graph from a connected, undirected,
positive-weight local reference graph $H_u=(V_u,E_u,w_u)$.  Fixing an
orientation, let $B$ be its vertex--edge incidence matrix, $W$ its diagonal
conductance matrix, and $L=BWB^\top$.  For an existing edge $e$ define
$\tau_e=w_e b_e^\top L^+b_e$.

\begin{proposition}[Weighted cut-space leverage]
$P=W^{1/2}B^\top L^+BW^{1/2}$ is the orthogonal projector onto
$\operatorname{im}(W^{1/2}B^\top)$. Hence $P_{ee}=\tau_e$,
$0<\tau_e\le 1$, and $\sum_e\tau_e=|V_u|-1$. Moreover, $\tau_e=1$ if and
only if $e$ is a bridge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Writing $A=BW^{1/2}$ gives $P=A^\top(AA^\top)^+A$. A thin singular-value
decomposition gives $P=V_rV_r^\top$, with $r=|V_u|-1$.  The diagonal and
trace identities follow.  Thomson's principle bounds the terminal
resistance by the direct-edge resistance $1/w_e$, with equality exactly
when no alternate path exists.
\end{proof}

For candidate $v$ let $z_v=(x_v-x_u)/\|x_v-x_u\|_2$, freeze
$r_v=\tau_{(u,v)}^{H_u}$ before selection, and set
$\ell_v=\exp[-d_X(u,v)^2/\rho_u^2]$.  For $\sigma>0$ define
\begin{equation}
 F_u(S)=\alpha\sum_{v\in S}r_v+
 \beta\log\det\!\left(I+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{v\in S}z_vz_v^\top\right)+
 \gamma\sum_{v\in S}\ell_v .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Frozen local objective]
For $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0$, $F_u$ is normalized, monotone, and
submodular. Under $|S|\le M$, exact greedy attains at least $1-1/e$ of the
optimal value of this frozen directed local-selection problem.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first and third terms are nonnegative modular functions. If
$A_S=I+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{v\in S}z_vz_v^\top$, the matrix determinant lemma
gives marginal gain
$\log(1+\sigma^{-2}z_v^\top A_S^{-1}z_v)$. For $S\subseteq T$,
$A_T^{-1}\preceq A_S^{-1}$, proving diminishing returns. The standard
cardinality-constrained monotone-submodular greedy theorem completes the
claim.
\end{proof}

The scope is deliberately local. Reciprocal insertion and reverse pruning
couple degree budgets at different vertices, so the proposition is not a
global approximation theorem for HNSW construction. Recomputing leverages
after every selection also changes the set function; a five-vertex
counterexample shows that the natural sum of recomputed selected-edge
leverages is not submodular.

Finally, resistance is not a navigation certificate. A bridge edge can point
strictly away from a query, while an edge with arbitrarily small leverage can
be the only distance-decreasing shortcut. Even arbitrarily accurate spectral
approximation need not preserve adjacency-based greedy paths. We therefore
treat leverage as a structural term and evaluate query progress separately.
