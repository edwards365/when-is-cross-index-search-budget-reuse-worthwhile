\section{Reproducibility and Operational Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{RQ9: Can the Decisions and Numbers Be Checked?}
The compact evidence package contains per-target certification failures, confidence allocations, selected shifts, paired evaluation arrays, cost components, fresh-query summaries, and generated table inputs. One script regenerates figures and typeset rows; another reanalyzes frozen native-response files through explicit repository and replay-root arguments. Historical records, the source-only boundary, the Faiss target-certified stage, and Deep1M confirmation retain separate evidence labels and role manifests.

The artifact audit preserves 80 pre-audit scientific files, passes a neutral-path smoke and 28 focused tests, and exposes guarded replay entry points for DARTH, Vamana, and Deep1M. In a clean Python environment built from the anonymous allowlist alone, the manuscript package passes 244 earlier evidence checks, reproduces generated table text, regenerates six figures, and verifies 210/210 source-only fresh-query checks. The post-seal checker validates 1,104 Faiss target decisions, 48 target summaries, four cost summaries, and both cost ledgers. The Deep1M checker covers all 56 target-certified decisions and their crossed-bootstrap, tail, and deletion summaries. A fresh Faiss environment also reproduces 6,000 native action rows for one registered SIFT build with zero top-$k$, recall, NDC, or action-order mismatch. Full native replay requires the separately provisioned vectors, truth, indexes, binaries, and role manifests listed in the supplement.

\paragraph{Operational decision.}
The evidence yields a three-step maintenance rule. First, treat an inherited budget as source evidence and measure its target response. Second, qualify the completed candidate/fallback procedure on target queries under a declared risk event. Third, compare its serving savings with the cost of the information used to construct and certify it. ICBA records these steps. TCP is appropriate when recurring-query histories exist and a target selection role can choose a shift. Fixed slack uses a lower-information candidate and target certification; its Faiss and Deep1M results show that this route can retain work, while endpoint clipping on the 100K hnswlib grids identifies a regime with no recoverable slack.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
The portability estimates are conditional on the registered builds, query populations, action grids, failure event, and SLA. TCP's endpoint-relative recovery is positive on two Faiss-HNSW refresh workloads, but the equal-information audit isolates incremental value beyond target-global calibration only on SIFT; Arxiv is mean-unresolved, tail-blocked, and materially grid-sensitive under that comparator. A 2.5\% risk-limit sensitivity also removes the SIFT incremental mean conclusion and leaves three Arxiv endpoints unqualified. Fixed-slack target qualification succeeds on two Faiss 100K blocks and one hnswlib Deep1M block; Vamana contributes directional transfer evidence under a different recorded event. The TCP ledger models cached and cold profile acquisition in NDC. The fixed-slack ledger covers target-stage acquisition, while historical source acquisition and end-to-end truth/control time remain unmeasured. The experiments establish response variation and qualified recovery, rather than edge-level causality, open-build-population coverage, or hardware-general latency gains.

\section{Conclusion}
Search-budget portability is an index-conditioned decision. Across graph-only rebuilds, source first-passing actions add substantial failure above target-specific references; after refresh, an unchanged recurring-query policy loses its old qualification. ICBA converts these observations into an auditable contract for candidate construction, target certification, fallback, evaluation, and cost. TCP recovers search work for recurring profiles, while fixed slack demonstrates target-certified recovery on Faiss and hnswlib Deep1M and exposes endpoint-clipped cases with no gain. The combined evidence shows how to audit reuse, recover work when target evidence supports it, and decide whether that recovery is worth acquiring.
