\section{Reproducibility and Operational Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{RQ9: Can the Decisions and Numbers Be Checked?}
The compact evidence package contains per-target certification failures, sample counts, both confidence allocations, selected shifts, paired evaluation arrays, cost components, and generated table inputs. One script regenerates figures and typeset rows; another reanalyzes the frozen native response files through explicit repository and replay-root arguments. It checks the original twenty target decisions before deriving the new sensitivities. Historical results remain separate from the new outputs.

The preceding artifact audit preserved 80 pre-audit scientific files, passed a neutral-path smoke and 28 focused tests, and exposed guarded replay entry points for DARTH, Vamana, and Deep1M. The present manuscript adds table/figure regeneration and decision-level checks. These are reference-environment checks, not independent replication. Recreating native searches still requires the separately provisioned vectors, truth, indexes, binaries, and role manifests. The short supplement gives the statistical recipe and twenty-target certificate table; the source package records which evidence files supply each claim.

\paragraph{Interpretation for index maintenance.}
The experiments suggest three different operator decisions. After a graph-only rebuild, a historical budget is evidence about a source response, not the new one. After refresh, test the unchanged policy on the target rather than interpreting a high mean recall as query-risk qualification. If recovery is considered, evaluate its completed candidate/fallback procedure and the horizon needed to pay for target information. ICBA makes these checks explicit; TCP demonstrates their interaction on a recurring-query workload.

\paragraph{Remaining boundaries.}
The graph-only result is a retrospective response diagnostic; TCP's positive recovery evidence is a Faiss-HNSW profile-reuse case on two 100K datasets. Deep1M extends response evidence, not TCP's economic claim. The certification sensitivity is post-hoc and per target. The experiments do not identify a causal navigation-edge mechanism, demonstrate an unseen-query predictor, or measure controlled wall-clock recovery. Dynamic graph repair and target-trained adaptive methods are alternative system choices, not defeated baselines. These gaps delimit where a follow-up experiment would add evidence rather than merely repeat the present audit.

\section{Conclusion}
Search budgets are part of an index's operational state. On unchanged collections, source-derived first-passing actions incur substantial additional failure across hnswlib and Faiss rebuilds. In a separate refresh setting, an unchanged history-pool policy deteriorates on both datasets, and target recalibration recovers useful search work. ICBA exposes the cost of making that recovery statistically qualified: stricter candidate/fallback composition accepts fewer policies, and complete profile accounting delays break-even. Portability, qualification, and economics are therefore separate decisions that should be made for the target index and the workload it will serve.
