\section{Reproducibility and Operational Implications}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{RQ9: Can the Decisions and Numbers Be Checked?}
The compact evidence package contains per-target certification failures, both confidence allocations, selected shifts, paired evaluation arrays, cost components, the fresh-query summary, and generated table inputs. One script regenerates figures and typeset rows; another reanalyzes frozen native-response files through explicit repository and replay-root arguments. It checks the original twenty target decisions before deriving the new sensitivities. Historical records and the prospective source-slack stage remain separately labeled.

The preceding artifact audit preserved 80 pre-audit scientific files, passed a neutral-path smoke and 28 focused tests, and exposed guarded replay entry points for DARTH, Vamana, and Deep1M. In a clean Python environment built from the anonymous allowlist alone, the manuscript package passes 244 evidence checks, reproduces all generated table text exactly, regenerates six figures, and verifies 210/210 fresh-query checks. It also includes the 96 frozen source actions, a sanitized preregistration summary, and the source-slack protocol. These are reference-environment checks, not independent native-search replication. Recreating native searches still requires separately provisioned vectors, truth, indexes, binaries, and role manifests. The short supplement gives the statistical recipe and detailed certificate tables; the package maps each claim to its compact evidence source.

\paragraph{Interpretation for index maintenance.}
The experiments suggest three operator decisions. After a graph-only rebuild, a historical budget is evidence about a source response, not the new one. After refresh, test the unchanged policy on the target rather than interpreting high mean recall as query-risk qualification. If recovery is considered, evaluate its completed candidate/fallback procedure and the horizon needed to pay for information. ICBA makes these checks explicit; TCP demonstrates target-calibrated recovery on a recurring workload, while the fresh-query bridge shows when a cheaper source-only rule is or is not sufficient.

\paragraph{Remaining boundaries.}
The graph-only result is a retrospective response diagnostic; TCP's positive evidence is a Faiss-HNSW recurring-profile case on two 100K datasets. The fresh-query bridge is prospective with respect to query roles but conditional on existing registered builds, and its positive economic result is specific to the registered Faiss action grid. Deep1M extends response evidence, not recovery economics. The certification sensitivity is post-hoc and per target. The experiments do not identify a causal navigation-edge mechanism, demonstrate a general unseen-query predictor, or measure controlled wall-clock recovery. Dynamic graph repair and target-trained adaptive methods are alternatives, not defeated baselines. These boundaries identify where follow-up work would add evidence rather than repeat the audit.

\section{Conclusion}
Search budgets are part of an index's operational state. On unchanged collections, source-derived first-passing actions incur substantial additional failure across hnswlib and Faiss rebuilds. After refresh, an unchanged recurring-query policy deteriorates and target recalibration recovers useful work. On fresh queries, a simpler source-certified slack rule transfers economically on the registered Faiss grids but not on the hnswlib grids. ICBA makes these outcomes comparable without conflating them: stricter qualification can increase fallback, and complete acquisition accounting can delay break-even. Portability, certification, and economic value must therefore be decided for the target index, implementation, action grid, and workload.
