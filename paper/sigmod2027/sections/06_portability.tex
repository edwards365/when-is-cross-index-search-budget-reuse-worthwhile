\subsection{RQ1: Does the Old Budget Remain Safe?}
\textbf{Question.} Does the old-build budget meet the 5\% query-failure target after refresh and rebuilding?
\textbf{Protocol.} We execute the nine-history TCP pool without target recalibration or fallback on each of the ten targets per dataset.
\textbf{Observation.} Direct reuse saves \WZeroSiftSourceGain\% of mean distance evaluations on SIFT and \WZeroArxivSourceGain\% on Arxiv, but target failure is \WZeroSiftSourceRisk\% and \WZeroArxivSourceRisk\%, respectively. The fixed endpoint has risks \WFiveSiftEndpointRisk\% and \WFiveArxivEndpointRisk\%.
\textbf{Conclusion.} Low serving cost does not make the reused policy safe under the registered target event. The available primary summary establishes failure of target qualification after the combined refresh/rebuild intervention; it does not quantify a source-to-target risk increase against a source-certified reference.

\subsection{RQ4: What Decision Does ICBA Change?}
\textbf{Question.} Does target auditing distinguish unsafe efficiency from a usable recovery action?
\textbf{Protocol.} The audited-reuse comparator checks the unshifted policy; the recovery comparator first selects a global shift on selection queries. Both then use the separate certification role and registered fallback rule.
\textbf{Observation.} Audited source reuse falls back on all ten targets of each dataset, retaining endpoint risk and zero serving savings. Recalibration accepts the candidate on ten SIFT and nine Arxiv targets; the remaining Arxiv target uses the endpoint. The completed decisions have empirical risks \WZeroSiftTargetRisk\% and \WZeroArxivTargetRisk\%.
\textbf{Conclusion.} The audit rules out direct reuse in these targets, while target selection recovers a lower-cost action in 19 of 20 cases. Candidate acceptance is an individual-CP outcome; its distinction from a joint 95\% pipeline certificate follows Section~\ref{sec:tcp-recalibration}.
