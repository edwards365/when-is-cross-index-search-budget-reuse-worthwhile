\section{Portability and the Audit Decision}
\label{sec:portability}

\subsection{RQ1: Does the Old Budget Remain Safe?}
\textbf{Question.} Does the old-build budget meet the 5\% query-failure target after refresh and rebuilding?
\textbf{Protocol.} We execute the nine-history TCP pool without target recalibration or fallback on each of the ten targets per dataset.
\textbf{Observation.} Direct reuse saves \WZeroSiftSourceGain\% of mean distance evaluations on SIFT-100K and \WZeroArxivSourceGain\% on Arxiv-Nomic-100K, but target failure is \WZeroSiftSourceRisk\% and \WZeroArxivSourceRisk\%, respectively. The fixed endpoint has risks \WFiveSiftEndpointRisk\% and \WFiveArxivEndpointRisk\%.
\textbf{Conclusion.} Low serving cost does not make the reused policy safe under the registered target event. The available primary summary establishes failure of target qualification after the combined refresh/rebuild intervention; it does not quantify a source-to-target risk increase against a source-certified reference.

\paragraph{Failure concentration.}
With 1,000 evaluation queries per target, direct-reuse risk exceeds 5\% on all ten builds of each dataset. Per-build risks span \WFiftyFiveSiftMinRisk--\WFiftyFiveSiftMaxRisk\% on SIFT-100K and \WFiftyFiveArxivMinRisk--\WFiftyFiveArxivMaxRisk\% on Arxiv-Nomic-100K. The largest-failure build contributes \WFiftyFiveSiftMaxShare\% and \WFiftyFiveArxivMaxShare\% of its dataset's failures; deleting it leaves risks of \WFiftyFiveSiftDeleteRisk\% and \WFiftyFiveArxivDeleteRisk\%. Figure~\ref{fig:decisions}(b) shows that failure is distributed across targets, not concentrated in one outlier. This descriptive check addresses build concentration; identifying the causal graph-topology mechanism is outside this replay's scope.

\subsection{RQ4: What Decision Does ICBA Change?}
\textbf{Question.} Does target auditing distinguish unsafe efficiency from a usable recovery action?
\textbf{Protocol.} The audited-reuse comparator checks the unshifted policy; the recovery comparator first selects a global shift on selection queries. Both then use the separate certification role and registered fallback rule.
\textbf{Observation.} Audited source reuse falls back on all ten targets of each dataset, retaining endpoint risk and zero serving savings. Recalibration accepts the candidate on ten SIFT-100K and nine Arxiv-Nomic-100K targets; the remaining target uses the endpoint. Figure~\ref{fig:decisions}(a) identifies each decision. The completed decisions have empirical risks \WZeroSiftTargetRisk\% and \WZeroArxivTargetRisk\%.
\textbf{Conclusion.} The audit rules out direct reuse in these targets, while target selection recovers a lower-cost action in 19 of 20 cases. Candidate acceptance is an individual-CP outcome; its distinction from a joint 95\% pipeline certificate follows Section~\ref{sec:tcp-recalibration}.
