\section{Recovery Quality and Build Sensitivity}
\label{sec:recovery-quality}

\subsection{RQ5: Can Target Recalibration Recover Value?}
\textbf{Question.} Can target-selection information recover search savings while meeting the empirical risk target?
\textbf{Protocol.} The recovery lane selects one global upward shift over the frozen source pool on 500 queries, checks it on a separate 500, and evaluates the resulting candidate/fallback decision on 1,000.
\textbf{Observation.} Relative to direct reuse, risk drops from \WZeroSiftSourceRisk\% to \WZeroSiftTargetRisk\% on SIFT and from \WZeroArxivSourceRisk\% to \WZeroArxivTargetRisk\% on Arxiv. Mean savings against the endpoint remain \WZeroSiftTargetGain\% and \WZeroArxivTargetGain\%. The registered gain intervals are [\WZeroSiftTargetGainLow, \WZeroSiftTargetGainHigh]\% and [\WZeroArxivTargetGainLow, \WZeroArxivTargetGainHigh]\%.
\textbf{Conclusion.} A target-selected global adjustment recovers empirical safety and substantial search savings on both recurring-profile workloads. It sacrifices some of direct reuse's unsafe savings to reduce failures.

\subsection{RQ6: Are Mean Gains Bought with a Worse Tail?}
\textbf{Question.} Does the completed recovery decision increase high-percentile distance counts?
\textbf{Protocol.} We compare pooled quantiles on identical evaluation queries, including the one endpoint-fallback target, against fixed-endpoint execution.
\textbf{Observation.} Both p95 and p99 are lower on both datasets (Table~\ref{tab:tail}). Fallback occurs on \WZeroSiftTargetFallbackBuilds/10 SIFT and \WZeroArxivTargetFallbackBuilds/10 Arxiv targets. On the Arxiv fallback target, the serving distribution equals that target's endpoint distribution by construction.
\textbf{Conclusion.} The observed pooled tails improve alongside the mean, without relying on widespread fallback. These pooled results support the registered descriptive tail criterion. They do not establish a separate nonfallback pooled-tail guarantee or a latency guarantee; per-build quantiles cannot be averaged to obtain either pooled quantile.

\subsection{RQ7: Does One Build Explain the Gain?}
\textbf{Question.} Does recovery depend on a single favorable target?
\textbf{Protocol.} We retain the registered nested bootstrap and recompute pooled gain after each target is omitted and after the highest relative-gain target is removed.
\textbf{Observation.} Minimum LOTO gains are \WFiveSiftLoto\% and \WFiveArxivLoto\%; delete-largest gains are \WFiveSiftDeleteLargest\% and \WFiveArxivDeleteLargest\% (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}). Both full-sample gain intervals have positive lower endpoints.
\textbf{Conclusion.} No single target explains the mean benefit within the ten registered builds. The wider Arxiv interval also makes the variability of its completed candidate/fallback decisions visible.
