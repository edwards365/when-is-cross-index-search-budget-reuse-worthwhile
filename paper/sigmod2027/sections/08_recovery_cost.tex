\section{RQ8: When Does Recovery Repay Its Cost?}
\label{sec:recovery}

\textbf{Question.} At what serving horizon does cheaper search repay the information used to obtain it?
\textbf{Protocol.} The frozen ledger expresses cost in distance evaluations:
\begin{align}
 C_\pi(N)&=C_{\mathrm{acquire},\pi}+N\bar c_\pi,\label{eq:cost}\\
 N_{\mathrm{BE}}&=\frac{\Delta C_{\mathrm{acquire}}}
 {\bar c_{\mathrm{ref}}-\bar c_\pi},\label{eq:breakeven}
\end{align}
where $\Delta C_{\mathrm{acquire}}$ is acquisition cost above the reference. There is no finite break-even if serving savings are nonpositive and this increment is positive. The recorded numerator includes old-build search profiles for the evaluation query set, all-grid target-selection searches, selected-candidate certification searches, and exact target truth for the 500 selection and 500 certification queries. Truth is modeled as a scan of 100,000 vectors per query. The graph rebuild is common to both choices. Control operations receive zero distance calls, which is not a zero-time assumption.

\textbf{Observation.} The dataset-level ratio of mean acquisition cost to mean serving savings gives \num{\WZeroSiftBreakEven}\ executions for SIFT-100K and \num{\WZeroArxivBreakEven}\ for Arxiv-Nomic-100K. All SIFT-100K targets have positive serving savings; one Arxiv-Nomic-100K target has zero savings and does not amortize. At $N=10^6$, the modeled net reductions average \num{\WZeroSiftNetSavingMillion}\ and \num{\WZeroArxivNetSavingMillion}\ distance evaluations per target (Table~\ref{tab:economics}).

\textbf{Interpretation.} The ledger does not separately charge old-profile truth, old-build profiling of selection/certification queries, or endpoint-certificate search. The reported break-even therefore applies when these omitted acquisitions are already available or cost-shared; otherwise it is optimistic. For an additional incremental cost $\Delta C\ge0$, the corrected threshold is $N_{\mathrm{BE}}+\Delta C/(\bar c_{\rm end}-\bar c_\pi)$ whenever the denominator is positive. The actual query-reuse rate determines how often a new source profile must be acquired. Distance counts also exclude hardware-dependent overheads such as data movement, lookup time, and training or control time.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Recovery has positive long-horizon value under the reported acquisition model, while short-lived or nonamortizing targets favor the endpoint. Full deployment economics requires pricing the omitted information for the intended query workload; the serving gain alone is insufficient.
