\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:methodology}
\sectionaim{Define the populations, decisions, and uncertainty units used by RQ1--RQ9.}

\paragraph{Primary refresh experiment.}
We evaluate SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K after a registered 5\% mixed deletion/insertion refresh and graph rebuild. Each dataset has ten target builds. For every target, 500 queries select a policy, a disjoint 500 certify it, and a disjoint 1,000 evaluate it. The failure event is $\rec@10<0.95$; because $k=10$, passing requires all ten true neighbors. The source-reuse and target-recalibration policies use the query-indexed historical profiles defined in Section~\ref{sec:auditor}; the fixed endpoint is the registered $ef=200$ action. Thus this experiment measures portability for recurring profiled queries after data refresh and rebuilding, not a zero-label path for arbitrary new queries.

\paragraph{Measurements and decision rule.}
For policy $\pi$, we report empirical evaluation risk, mean distance evaluations, query-pooled p95 and p99 distance evaluations, certification outcome, and fallback. Relative search gain is
\begin{equation}
 g(\pi)=1-\frac{\bar c_\pi}{\bar c_{\rm end}},
 \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
where $\bar c_{\rm end}$ is the fixed-endpoint mean. ICBA admits a candidate only after isolated selection and certification; a failed candidate uses the registered endpoint. Candidate and endpoint bounds are individual one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson checks. We therefore report the resulting empirical deployment decision without upgrading their adaptive combination to a joint 95\% certificate.

\paragraph{Uncertainty and robustness.}
Target build is the outer unit. The registered nested bootstrap samples builds and then queries within sampled builds (5,000 replicates); leave-one-target-out (LOTO) and deletion of the largest-gain build test concentration. Repeated query IDs across builds mean that the interval is the output of this registered procedure, not a universal population interval. Cross-method, Vamana, and Deep1M blocks retain their own events and references; we use them to test recurrence, not to construct a pooled leaderboard.
