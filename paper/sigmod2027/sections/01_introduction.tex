\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\sectionaim{Connect an index-maintenance operation to a measurable portability problem.}

A search budget is chosen for a query and executed on a particular graph. When an index is rebuilt, its traversal paths can change even when its search interface remains unchanged. Reusing an earlier budget therefore raises a separate question from the quality of the new index: does the old decision still deliver the required recall at an acceptable cost?

This question matters at the boundary between index maintenance and query serving. An operator may retain a calibrated budget to avoid profiling costs, recalibrate it using target information, or use a larger fixed budget. These choices trade search work against information acquisition and statistical assurance. Their relative value depends on both the rebuilt graph and the expected serving horizon.

Our organizing insight is that budget portability is a property of a \emph{source policy, target build, and query workload}, rather than a property of a budget value alone. ICBA makes this dependence explicit. The empirical study follows three steps: establish transfer failures, examine their scope across implementations and workloads, and measure recovery with its information and cost requirements exposed. TCP enters as a recovery case study within this analysis.

\paragraph{Contributions to develop.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A build-conditioned formulation of budget transfer that distinguishes absolute target failure from excess failure relative to a reference.
\item An auditable evaluation workflow linking query roles, failure events, certification decisions, and cost accounting.
\item A layered empirical analysis of portability across budget policies, graph families, and data scale, with explicit boundaries between protocols.
\item A TCP recalibration case study showing where target information recovers search efficiency and where certification or acquisition costs constrain its use.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Four findings guiding the paper.}
First, direct reuse in the frozen TCP profile replay exceeds the 5\% empirical-risk target on both primary datasets. Second, the registered external-method bridges and Vamana audit provide additional transfer-failure observations under their own protocols. Third, target-selection recalibration reduces empirical failure while preserving distance-count savings in the primary replay. Fourth, recovery has a workload-dependent acquisition cost: a favorable per-query search result does not by itself establish lifecycle savings. Sections~\ref{sec:portability}--\ref{sec:recovery} place the evidence behind these findings.
