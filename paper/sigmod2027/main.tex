\title[Budget Transfer Across Graph-ANNS Rebuilds]{When Safe Search Budgets Do Not Transfer Across Graph-ANNS Rebuilds: [Experiments \& Analysis]}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Rebuilding a graph index can change the search procedure on which an approximate nearest-neighbor search budget was calibrated. We study when a source-build budget retains its recall target on a rebuilt index, how this transfer should be audited, and what target-side recovery costs. ICBA organizes the analysis around build-conditioned risk, isolated selection and certification roles, and explicit search-cost accounting. In a SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K query-profile replay after 5\% data refresh and rebuilding, direct TCP pool reuse has empirical target failure rates of \WZeroSiftSourceRisk\% and \WZeroArxivSourceRisk\%. Target-selection recalibration reduces these rates to \WZeroSiftTargetRisk\% and \WZeroArxivTargetRisk\%, with mean distance-count reductions of \WZeroSiftTargetGain\% and \WZeroArxivTargetGain\% relative to the registered fixed endpoint. Cross-family and scale analyses extend the evidence beyond this recovery case. The central systems implication is to treat a rebuilt index as a new calibration environment: measure portability, separate observed recovery from a valid certificate, and account for the cost of obtaining target information.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{approximate nearest-neighbor search, graph indexes, index rebuilding, risk auditing, budget transfer}
