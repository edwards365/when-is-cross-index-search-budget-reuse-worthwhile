\title[Auditing Search-Budget Portability]{Auditing Search-Budget Portability Across Graph-ANNS Rebuilds: [Experiments \& Analysis]}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Graph indexes are rebuilt to maintain search quality, yet the budget policy used to serve queries is often treated as reusable state. We audit that assumption through controlled rebuilds, a 5\% data-refresh case, and two fresh-query recovery stages. On unchanged SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K collections, transferring retrospective first-passing budgets across hnswlib and Faiss builds adds 17.17--23.60 percentage points of query failure relative to target-specific references. Index-Conditioned Budget Audit (ICBA) separates response evidence, executable-policy risk, distinctly scoped certification, and acquisition cost. After refresh, an unchanged recurring-query policy rises from 3.77\% to 6.69\% failure on SIFT and from 3.19\% to 7.24\% on Arxiv-Nomic. Target-calibrated pooling (TCP) retains jointly controlled mean-distance reductions of 44.39\% and 29.86\%, with cold-history break-even near 396,000 and 457,000 executions. A post-seal replication then fixes a source-derived one-rung candidate, certifies it on 500 target queries, and evaluates it on 500 disjoint queries over 552 Faiss source--target directions per dataset. All 1,104 candidates qualify; empirical risks are 0.13\% and 0.53\%, mean-distance savings are 8.28\% and 29.08\%, and pooled p95 does not increase. Target-stage acquisition amortizes after about 66,900/17,000 executions per isolated SIFT/Arxiv direction, or 3,019/755 when one target audit is shared across 23 source histories. Historical source-policy acquisition remains unmeasured, so these are not complete cold-lifecycle thresholds.
\end{abstract}
