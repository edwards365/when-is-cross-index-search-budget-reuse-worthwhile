\title[Auditing Search-Budget Portability]{Auditing Search-Budget Portability Across Graph-ANNS Rebuilds: [Experiments \& Analysis]}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}
\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
Graph indexes are rebuilt to maintain search quality, yet the budget policy used to serve queries is often treated as reusable state. We audit that assumption through controlled rebuilds, a 5\% data-refresh case, and a fresh-query recovery test. On unchanged SIFT-100K and Arxiv-Nomic-100K collections, transferring retrospective first-passing budgets across hnswlib and Faiss builds adds 17.17--23.60 percentage points of query failure relative to target-specific references. Index-Conditioned Budget Audit (ICBA) separates response evidence from executable-policy risk, separate certification, and acquisition cost. After refresh, an unchanged recurring-query policy rises from 3.77\% to 6.69\% failure on SIFT and from 3.19\% to 7.24\% on Arxiv-Nomic. Target-calibrated pooling (TCP) retains jointly controlled mean-distance reductions of 44.39\% and 29.86\%, with cold-history break-even near 396,000 and 457,000 executions. In a separately preregistered fresh-query test, all 24 Faiss source decisions per dataset are individually certified and evaluated over 552 directed source--target pairs. Their one-rung policies save 8.27\% and 29.09\% mean distance work at held-out empirical risks of 0.18\% and 0.67\%; endpoint clipping leaves no serving-work saving on either hnswlib dataset. Thus portability, qualification, serving-work savings, and net economic recovery are distinct target-dependent decisions.
\end{abstract}
